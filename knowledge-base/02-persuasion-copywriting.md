# PERSUASION COPYWRITING — Landing Page Agent Knowledge Layer

Reference text. Every section is built to be applied directly to a hero, section, or component while writing. Formulas use `[BRACKETS]` = fill-in slots.

---

## 1. AWARENESS STAGES (Schwartz) — the master variable

The single biggest determinant of what the headline says is **how much the visitor already knows**. Match the copy to the stage the traffic arrives in, or the page bounces. Rule: **the colder the traffic, the higher up the funnel the headline must start, and the longer the lead must be.**

| Stage | What they know | What the HEADLINE must lead with | Lead length | Typical source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Don't know they have the problem | A story, a startling stat, an identity call-out, or a trend. NEVER the product. | Long (story/stat lead) | Cold Meta/TikTok interruption, broad interest targeting |
| **Problem-aware** | Feel the pain, don't know solutions exist | Name the pain back to them, vividly | Medium (problem lead) | Cold Meta, problem-keyword content, TikTok pain-point hooks |
| **Solution-aware** | Know solution types exist, comparing categories | The result/outcome + your mechanism as the better way | Medium (promise lead) | Mid-intent Google ("how to fix X"), Meta consideration |
| **Product-aware** | Know YOUR product, not convinced | Differentiation, proof, offer, risk-reversal | Short (offer lead) | Branded Google search, retargeting, comparison keywords |
| **Most-aware** | Ready, need the deal/nudge | The offer + CTA, price, urgency | Shortest (pure offer) | Branded search, cart abandon retargeting, email click |

### Hero shifts by stage (the practical rewrite)

Same product (a lawn/turf install) across stages:

- **Unaware hero:** *"The average Vegas homeowner pours 60,000 gallons of water on grass that dies anyway."* (stat/story lead — problem isn't even "turf," it's wasted money/water)
- **Problem-aware hero:** *"Sick of a brown, patchy lawn you can't keep alive in the desert?"* (mirror the pain)
- **Solution-aware hero:** *"Artificial turf that looks real, drains fast, and never needs water — installed in 3 days."* (outcome + mechanism vs. the category)
- **Product-aware hero:** *"Why 2,000+ Vegas homeowners chose US Turf over the other guys — 15-yr warranty, no subcontractors."* (differentiation + proof)
- **Most-aware hero:** *"$500 off your install this month. Free quote in 60 seconds."* (offer + CTA)

### Traffic-source mapping (default assumptions when brief is silent)

- **Cold Meta / TikTok** → assume **unaware → problem-aware**. Long-form lead, story/pain hook, disruptive first line. Do NOT open with the product name. Message-match the *ad hook*, not the brand.
- **High-intent Google Search** → assume **solution-aware → product-aware**. Message-match the exact **keyword/query**; headline should echo the search phrase. Lead with outcome + mechanism.
- **Branded / bottom-funnel Google** → **most-aware**. Lead with offer, price, CTA, trust.
- **Retargeting** → **product-aware → most-aware**. They've met you. Lead with objection-crusher + risk reversal + offer. Reference the prior visit implicitly ("Still thinking it over?").
- **Email click** → **most-aware**. The email already sold; the LP closes. Repeat the email's promise verbatim (message match) and put the CTA above the fold.

**Non-negotiable:** LP headline must match the **ad/keyword/email** that produced the click. Message-mismatch is the #1 cause of paid-traffic bounce.

## 2. MARKET SOPHISTICATION (5 levels) — how hard the claim has to work

Sophistication = how many similar claims the market has already heard. As competitors pile on, the *same claim stops working* and you must escalate.

| Level | Market state | What to do with the claim | Example (weight loss) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | First to market, no competition | State the plain claim/benefit directly | "Lose weight" |
| **2** | Competitors copying, claims still fresh | Enlarge/one-up the claim (bigger, faster, easier) | "Lose 30 lbs in 30 days" |
| **3** | Market claim-fatigued, disbelief | Introduce a **mechanism** — the unique *how* that makes the claim believable again | "Burn fat with the GLP-1 metabolic switch" |
| **4** | Mechanism war, everyone has one | Enlarge/elaborate the mechanism (better/simpler/proprietary version) | "The 6-second morning ritual that flips the switch" |
| **5** | Total skepticism/exhaustion | Pivot to **identity, experience, and connection** — sell who they are, the feeling, the brand story; claim goes implicit | "For the woman done with diets. This is different." |

**Practical use:** Before writing a headline, ask *"How many competitors is this prospect comparing us against, and how tired are they of the standard promise?"*
- Low sophistication (new/niche category) → make the direct benefit claim boldly.
- High sophistication (insurance, mattresses, CBD, weight loss, solar, agencies) → **do not** repeat the category claim. Lead with **mechanism** (L3–4) or **identity/experience** (L5). A unique mechanism ("proprietary 3-layer drainage core") re-earns belief the bare claim has lost.

## 3. CORE COPY FRAMEWORKS — with when-to-use

Pick the framework by **awareness stage + emotional temperature + section length**.

**PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve**
Structure: name pain → twist the knife (consequences, cost of inaction, emotion) → present product as relief.
Use for: **problem-aware** cold traffic, remarketing, hero + first section, short ads. Fastest emotional grip. Default for Meta cold.

**AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action**
Attention (hook) → Interest (relevant facts/story) → Desire (benefits, proof, transformation) → Action (CTA).
Use for: full long-form landing pages, sales pages, the *whole-page skeleton*. The macro-structure most LPs sit inside.

**BAB — Before, After, Bridge**
Current painful state → desired future state → the product as the bridge.
Use for: **transformation** offers, brand/awareness, storytelling heroes, video scripts. Great when the *after* is emotionally vivid (fitness, home improvement, financial).

**4Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push**
Promise (bold benefit) → Picture (paint them living it) → Proof (evidence it's real) → Push (CTA + urgency).
Use for: **solution/product-aware** LPs, sales letters, webinar pages. Strong when you have real proof assets. Excellent full-page skeleton alternative to AIDA when proof is abundant.

**FAB — Feature, Advantage, Benefit**
Feature → what it does/advantage → what it means for them.
Use for: **feature/spec sections**, comparison tables, B2B/SaaS, product-aware buyers who need to justify. Not a hero framework — a *body-section* framework.

**PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story/Solution, Transformation, Offer, Response**
Problem → Amplify stakes → Story + Solution (testimonial/origin) → Transformation (outcome + proof) → Offer (full stack, price, bonuses, guarantee) → Response (CTA).
Use for: **long-form sales pages**, high-ticket, coaching/info products, anything needing a complete narrative arc from cold to close on one page.

**Star-Story-Solution**
Star (relatable protagonist — customer or founder) → Story (their struggle + turning point) → Solution (what changed it = your product).
Use for: **founder/origin stories, case-study sections, UGC/VSL scripts, unaware traffic** where you can't lead with product. Builds trust through narrative before the pitch.

**Selection cheat:**
- Cold + emotional → PAS or BAB
- Whole page → AIDA or 4Ps (proof-heavy) or PASTOR (high-ticket)
- Body/feature sections → FAB
- Trust/story blocks → Star-Story-Solution

## 4. HEADLINE FORMULAS

**80% read the headline, 20% read the body. Write 10–25, ship 1.** A headline must do at least one: promise a benefit, provoke curiosity, make a bold claim, or call out the audience. Best headlines stack two.

### The core pattern: OUTCOME + EMOTION (+ TIMEFRAME + REMOVE-OBJECTION)

`[Desirable outcome] + [emotional payoff or how it feels] + [specific timeframe] + [without the thing they dread]`

- "Get a lawn the whole street envies — in 3 days, without ever watering again."
- "Book 5 more jobs a week — finally off the tools by 5pm — without spending a dollar more on ads."

The four levers: **Outcome** (what they get), **Emotion** (why they care), **Time** (how fast = believability + urgency), **Objection removal** ("without ___" kills the top fear inside the promise).

### 10 proven templates (fill-in-the-blank)

1. **How-to:** `How to [achieve desired outcome] without [pain/objection]`
   → "How to sell your house in 14 days without a single showing."
2. **Number/list:** `[#] [ways/reasons/secrets] to [outcome] (even if [obstacle])`
   → "7 ways to cut your energy bill in half (even in an all-electric home)."
3. **They-said/proof:** `They [doubted/laughed] when [X] — until [surprising result]`
   → "They said desert grass was impossible. Then 2,000 homeowners proved them wrong."
4. **Question (pain mirror):** `Struggling with [specific pain]? Here's [the fix]`
   → "Tired of leads that ghost you? Here's the 60-second fix."
5. **Direct benefit + timeframe:** `[Outcome] in [timeframe] — [risk reversal]`
   → "A booked calendar in 30 days — or you don't pay."
6. **Audience call-out:** `Attention [specific audience]: [benefit/warning]`
   → "Attention Vegas homeowners: your water bill is about to jump 12%."
7. **Vs./better-way:** `The [smarter/faster] way to [outcome] — no [old painful method]`
   → "The smarter way to get artificial turf — no pushy salesmen, no subcontractors."
8. **If-then / capability:** `If you can [tiny effort], you can [big outcome]`
   → "If you can send a text, you can run these ads."
9. **Curiosity gap:** `The [surprising thing] behind [desirable result] (that [authority] won't tell you)`
   → "The one drainage layer behind turf that never floods (that most installers skip)."
10. **Testimonial-as-headline:** `"[Verbatim customer quote with a number]" — [Name, descriptor]`
    → "\"Cut my water bill from $340 to $90.\" — Maria, Henderson NV"

Bonus — **Ridiculously specific claim:** `[Exact number/detail] [outcome]` → "Installed in 3 days. Warrantied for 15 years. Zero watering, forever."

### Headline rules

- **Length:** aim 6–12 words for the LP H1 (Google ad headlines 30 chars). Shorter = scannable; specific > clever.
- **Specificity beats hype:** "Save $2,140/yr" beats "Save big." Round numbers feel invented; odd/precise numbers feel real ("$2,137" > "$2,000").
- **Numbers/data:** include at least one concrete number when credible — it stops scrolls and signals proof.
- **Power words (use sparingly, 1 per headline):** free, new, proven, guaranteed, finally, instant, because, you, results, secret, avoid, mistake, worst, without, now. "You/your" is the highest-converting word — write to one person.
- **Cut:** adverbs, "we/our" openings, jargon (unless audience uses it natively), and any word that doesn't add meaning. Kill "-ly" words. Front-load the benefit.
- **5-second test:** a stranger must grasp the core promise in 5 seconds.

## 5. HERO / SUBHEAD STRUCTURE — the Promise–Proof–Path pattern

The above-the-fold hero should resolve three questions in order: *What is this? Is it for real? What do I do?*

```
[EYEBROW/KICKER]   – audience or category tag, 2–5 words (optional)
[H1 HEADLINE]      – the PROMISE (outcome + emotion, §4)
[SUBHEAD]          – the PATH + specifics: how it works / who it's for / why believe (15–25 words)
[PROOF STRIP]      – rating, # served, logos, or a one-line credibility stat
[PRIMARY CTA]      – benefit-driven button (§9)
[RISK REVERSAL]    – microcopy under button killing the #1 friction ("Free. No card. 60 sec.")
[HERO VISUAL]      – shows the AFTER state / product in use, with directional cue toward CTA
```

**Promise → Proof → Path in one hero:**
- **Promise (H1):** "A lawn that stays green all year — without a drop of water."
- **Proof (strip):** "★★★★★ 2,000+ installs across Las Vegas · 15-year warranty"
- **Path (subhead):** "Premium artificial turf, installed by our own crews in as little as 3 days. Get a free, no-pressure quote today."
- **Path (CTA):** "Get My Free Quote"

**Subhead job:** the H1 makes the emotional promise; the **subhead makes it believable and concrete** — it answers "how" or "why" and introduces the mechanism (critical at sophistication L3+). Never let the subhead just restate the H1.

**Above-the-fold non-negotiables:** value prop visible without scrolling; CTA visible without scrolling; one proof element near the CTA; message-match to the ad/keyword.

## 6. FEATURE → BENEFIT → MEANING translation

Buyers don't buy features; they buy a better version of their life. Translate every feature down two levels.

- **Feature:** what it is/has (factual)
- **Benefit:** what that does for them (practical/emotional)
- **Meaning / Outcome:** what their life looks like after — concrete, aspirational, sensory

**Method — the "So what?" ladder:** state the feature, ask "so what does that mean for them?" until you hit an emotion or a life scene. Stop at the level of meaning.

**Worked example:**
| Feature | So what? (Benefit) | So what? (Meaning/Outcome) |
|---|---|---|
| 15-year warranty | You're covered for the life of the lawn | "Install it once and never think about your yard again — for 15 years." |
| Multi-layer drainage core | Water drains fast, no pooling | "Kids and dogs back on the grass 10 minutes after a storm — no mud tracked through the house." |
| Own install crews (no subs) | Consistent quality, accountable | "One team, start to finish, standing behind their own work." |

**Copy rule:** lead sections with the **meaning/outcome**, use the **benefit** as the bridge, and cite the **feature** as *proof/evidence* underneath. Order on the page: Outcome headline → benefit sentence → feature as supporting bullet. Features alone convince no one; features *attached to meaning* are what close.

**Formula:** `[Feature] so you can [benefit], which means [outcome scene].`

## 7. VOICE-OF-CUSTOMER (VoC) MINING — steal their words

The highest-converting copy is **not written — it's assembled from the customer's own language.** Your job is transcription, not invention.

### Where to pull language
- **Reviews** (Google, Yelp, Amazon, Trustpilot, G2) — especially **3-star reviews** (most balanced: real pros + real objections).
- **Sales call recordings / Granola transcripts** — verbatim pain phrasing and objections.
- **Support tickets, chat logs, DMs** — the exact words for problems.
- **Competitor reviews** — what people wish the alternative did (gaps = your angles).
- **Reddit / Facebook groups / forum threads** — unfiltered, un-marketed language.
- **Post-purchase surveys** — "What almost stopped you from buying?" (objections) and "What convinced you?" (winning benefits).
- **Search queries** — the literal phrasing of their problem.

### Turn reviews into copy (process)
1. **Harvest 20–50 reviews**, paste raw.
2. **Tag by theme:** desired outcomes, pains, objections, "aha" moments, emotional words, the exact metaphors they use.
3. **Extract verbatim phrases** — keep their nouns and verbs. "It just looks so real" beats "photorealistic aesthetic."
4. **Map to page slots:**
   - Recurring **outcome** phrase → headline/subhead
   - Recurring **pain** phrase → PAS problem line / ad hook
   - Recurring **objection** → FAQ + risk-reversal microcopy
   - High-emotion quote **with a number** → pull-quote / testimonial block (verbatim)
5. **Mine the "before" state:** reviews that say "I used to..." give you the BAB *Before* in the customer's own words.

**Rule:** if 5+ customers use the same phrase, put it in the headline. Their words test-beat your clever ones nearly every time. Preserve grammar/voice — polished testimonials read fake.

## 8. OBJECTION HANDLING & FAQ PSYCHOLOGY

Every unaddressed objection is a silent exit. **Surface objections on purpose and neutralize them where they arise.**

### Find the top objections
The universal five: **Price** (too expensive / not worth it), **Trust** (will they deliver / is this legit), **Relevance/fit** (will it work for *my* situation), **Effort/risk** (hard to use / what if it fails), **Timing** (why now vs. later). Pull the client-specific versions from VoC ("What almost stopped you?").

### Neutralize (tactics by objection)
- **Price:** reframe as cost-per-day, compare to cost of the problem/inaction, anchor against a higher number, break into terms, or stack value above price. "Less than your monthly water bill."
- **Trust:** proof — reviews, warranty, guarantee, credentials, real names/photos, years in business, "our own crews."
- **Relevance:** specificity + segmentation. "Built for [exact audience/situation]." Show a case study matching them.
- **Effort/risk:** risk-reversal — money-back guarantee, free trial, "no card," "cancel anytime," done-for-you framing.
- **Timing:** cost of delay + ethical urgency (real deadline, real limited capacity).

### Placement & ordering
- Handle the **#1 objection inside the hero** (as risk-reversal microcopy under the CTA) — don't make them scroll to feel safe.
- Address objections **at the moment they arise**: price objection next to the pricing/CTA; "will it work for me" next to the feature it doubts.
- **FAQ ordering:** most-common/most-blocking first. Never bury the real dealbreaker at #8. Order roughly: fit → price/terms → trust/guarantee → logistics → edge cases.
- **FAQ psychology:** questions are objections in disguise — phrase each as the customer's real worry ("Is this going to look fake?"), answer directly, then add a micro-benefit. Don't use FAQs for keyword stuffing; use them to close doubt. A confident, specific answer doubles as proof.
- **Reframe, don't dismiss:** validate the concern ("Fair question —") then dissolve it. Never argue with the prospect.

**FAQ answer formula:** `[Direct yes/no] + [reason/proof] + [reassuring micro-benefit].`
→ "Does it look fake? No — our turf uses multi-tone fibers and a thatch layer, which is why most guests can't tell until they touch it."

## 9. MICROCOPY — the small words that convert

### CTA button copy
- **Pattern:** `[Action verb] + [benefit/possessive]`. First-person + benefit outperforms generic. Use "Get/Start/Claim/Book/Send," not "Submit."
- **Winners:** "Get My Free Quote" · "Start Saving Today" · "Claim My 20% Off" · "Book My Free Call" · "Send Me the Guide" · "See My Custom Plan"
- **Losers:** "Submit," "Click Here," "Learn More" (dead), "Sign Up" (cost-framed).
- **Rules:** 2–5 words. Restate the value, not the mechanic. **"My/Me" > "Your"** on buttons (the user's inner voice). Match the button to what actually happens next (no surprise). One primary CTA color, repeated down the page with the *same words*.

### Form labels & fields
- Label the **outcome**, not the task: form heading "Get your free quote," not "Contact form."
- **Minimize fields** — each field drops conversion. Ask only what you'll use now.
- Labels above fields (not placeholder-only — placeholders vanish and hurt a11y).
- Reassure at the point of friction: under email → "No spam, ever." under phone → "So we can send your quote — no cold calls."

### Trust lines (microcopy under CTAs/forms)
- `Free · No credit card · Takes 60 seconds`
- `Join 2,000+ Las Vegas homeowners`
- `100% money-back guarantee · Cancel anytime`
- `Your info is secure. We never share it.`
Place directly under the button — this is where the last hesitation lives.

### Error / validation states
- Human, specific, blameless, with the fix: "That email's missing an @ — mind checking it?" not "Invalid input."
- Never lose their data on error. Keep it inline, next to the field, in real time.
- Success confirmation should sell the next step: "You're in! Check your inbox for your quote (arrives in ~2 min)."

## 10. SOCIAL PROOF & ETHICAL URGENCY/SCARCITY

### Social proof patterns (strongest → weakest)
1. **Specific quantified testimonial** (name + photo + number + outcome): "\"Saved $250/mo.\" — Maria G., Henderson"
2. **Number served / volume:** "2,000+ installs across Las Vegas."
3. **Star rating + review count:** "★★★★★ 4.9 from 480 reviews."
4. **Named results/case study:** before→after with a metric.
5. **Authority/logos, certifications, press.**
6. **Similarity proof:** "Homeowners in *your* neighborhood."
7. **Wisdom of crowd / trending:** "Our most-requested package."

**Copy rules:** proof must be **specific and attributed** — vague/anonymous praise reads as fake and can lower trust. Put proof **next to the CTA** (the decision point) and **next to the claim it supports**. Numbers > adjectives. Use the customer's verbatim words (§7). Real names, real photos.

### Ethical urgency & scarcity (must be TRUE)
Urgency works only when honest; fake countdowns destroy trust and invite legal risk.
- **Real deadline:** "Offer ends Sunday" (and it actually does).
- **Real capacity limit:** "We take 8 installs a month — 3 spots left in July."
- **Seasonal/logistical truth:** "Book before peak season or wait 6 weeks."
- **Cost of delay (always ethical):** "Every month you wait is another $180 water bill."
- **Legitimate stock/enrollment counts** tied to real inventory.
**Never:** evergreen fake countdown timers, invented "only 2 left" on unlimited digital goods, false "prices rise tonight." If the scarcity isn't real, use **cost-of-inaction** framing instead — it creates urgency without a lie.

## 11. READABILITY & LOW-COGNITIVE-LOAD RULES

Confused visitors don't convert. Make comprehension effortless.

- **Grade level: 5th–7th grade.** Test with Flesch–Kincaid / Hemingway. Write like you talk.
- **Sentence length:** average under 15 words; vary rhythm; open sections with a short punchy line. One idea per sentence.
- **Paragraphs:** 1–3 sentences max. White space is a feature. No walls of text on mobile.
- **Words:** short, common, concrete. "Use" not "utilize." Cut jargon unless the audience speaks it natively. Kill adverbs and filler.
- **Scannability:** the page must make sense **read only by headers, subheads, bold phrases, and CTAs.** 80% skim. Front-load meaning in every subhead. Bold the phrase that carries the point.
- **One-hook / one-number / one-CTA per view** (Kyle's low-cognitive-load rule): each screen/section carries **one** main message, **one** headline number, **one** ask. No math, no asterisks, no multi-step inference. **2-second comprehension test** per section.
- **One primary CTA per page** (repeated), no competing asks. Remove nav/exit links on dedicated LPs.
- **Directional flow:** each section pushes the eye to the next; visual cues point to the CTA.
- **Active voice, "you" voice, present tense.** "You get" not "customers will be provided."
- **Read it aloud:** if you stumble, rewrite. If it sounds like a brochure, it's wrong.

**Final gate before shipping any section:** (1) Does a stranger get the promise in 5 seconds? (2) Is there exactly one message, one number, one CTA? (3) Does it match the traffic's awareness stage and the ad that drove the click? (4) Are the words the *customer's* words? If any answer is no, rewrite.
