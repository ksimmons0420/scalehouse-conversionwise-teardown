# CONVERSION PSYCHOLOGY & CRO MECHANICS
## Reference Layer for Scale House Landing Page Agent

Format per principle: **Principle** (one line) → **Why (cognitive mechanism)** → **On-page application + placement**.

---

## 1. PERSUASION & COGNITIVE BIASES

**Social Proof** — People copy the actions of similar others when uncertain.
- Why: Under ambiguity the brain outsources the decision to the crowd as a low-cost heuristic ("if many chose this, it's safe").
- Apply: Star-rating cluster + review count in hero and next to every CTA; testimonial wall mid-page; "Joined by 12,400 homeowners" counter; logo bar of recognizable customers. Placement: one proof unit within the hero, a dense block immediately after the primary CTA, and a micro-proof line beside the final CTA. Similarity beats volume — match the testimonial's face/role/geo to the visitor.

**Authority** — People defer to credible experts and legitimate credentials.
- Why: Cognitive shortcut — expertise signals lower risk of a bad decision, offloading evaluation effort.
- Apply: Certifications, licenses, "Board-certified," founder credentials, press logos, expert quote with headshot + title. Placement: trust bar under the hero and a credibility block adjacent to the offer/price. For regulated verticals (medical, legal, home services) this outranks social proof.

**Scarcity** — Limited supply raises perceived value.
- Why: Scarcity is a proxy for desirability and triggers anticipated-regret / loss aversion.
- Apply: Real stock counts ("4 install slots left this month"), limited cohort size. Placement: beside price and at the CTA. MUST be genuine — fake scarcity that resets on refresh destroys trust and is a legal risk.

**Urgency** — A deadline compresses deliberation into action.
- Why: Time pressure short-circuits over-analysis and raises the cost of inaction now vs. later.
- Apply: Countdown to a real deadline, "Book by Friday for July install," expiring bonus. Placement: sticky bar top-of-page + at CTA. Pair with scarcity but never fabricate.

**Reciprocity** — People repay value received.
- Why: Deep social norm; unreciprocated gifts create discomfort the brain resolves by giving back.
- Apply: Free tool, instant quote, downloadable guide, free sample/audit given BEFORE the ask. Placement: lead magnet as a mid-funnel micro-conversion; frame the free thing as a genuine gift, not a gated bribe.

**Commitment & Consistency** — Small yes's lead to bigger yes's.
- Why: People act consistently with prior self-perception; a small commitment rewrites self-image ("I'm someone doing this").
- Apply: Multi-step forms that ask an easy, low-threat question first (zip code, "What's your goal?") before contact fields. Placement: split any long form into a 2–3 step wizard; the first step is the commitment lever.

**Liking** — People buy from those they like and relate to.
- Why: Affinity + familiarity lower perceived risk; we assume liked parties won't harm us.
- Apply: Real human faces (founder, team, customers like the visitor), warm conversational copy, shared-values language. Placement: hero human imagery, "our story" strip, testimonial faces matched to audience.

**Unity** — Shared identity ("we") is stronger than mere liking.
- Why: In-group membership makes the offer feel like it belongs to "people like me," not an outsider selling.
- Apply: Identity language ("Built for Texas contractors," "For busy moms who…"). Placement: headline and sub-headline; audience-mirroring imagery in hero.

**Loss Aversion** — Losses hurt ~2x more than equivalent gains please.
- Why: Asymmetric prospect-theory weighting; the brain overweights potential loss.
- Apply: Frame around what they keep losing by waiting ("Every month without X costs you $Y"), "Don't lose your spot." Placement: near CTA and in urgency messaging. Balance with aspirational gain framing so tone stays positive.

**Anchoring** — First number seen frames all later judgments.
- Why: Insufficient adjustment from an initial reference point.
- Apply: Show highest/original price first, then the offer price; lead pricing with the premium tier. Placement: pricing section, strike-through original next to sale price.

**Framing** — The same fact persuades differently by wording.
- Why: Choices are evaluated against a reference frame, not in absolute terms.
- Apply: "90% fat-free" vs "10% fat"; "$1/day" vs "$30/mo"; "Save 40%" vs "$400 off." Placement: price presentation, benefit bullets, guarantee copy.

**Decoy Effect** — A third, asymmetrically-dominated option steers choice.
- Why: Adding an inferior-to-one-option decoy makes the target look objectively superior, easing the decision.
- Apply: 3-tier pricing where the middle/target tier is clearly the best value vs. a deliberately weak neighbor. Placement: pricing table; highlight the target as "Most Popular."

**Endowment Effect** — People value what they feel they already own.
- Why: Ownership raises valuation; giving it up registers as a loss.
- Apply: "Your plan," "Your quote," free trials, configurators/customizers that create pre-ownership. Placement: form results ("Here's YOUR estimate"), trial CTAs.

**Zeigarnik Effect** — Unfinished tasks nag until completed.
- Why: Open loops occupy working memory and create tension seeking closure.
- Apply: Progress bars on multi-step forms, "You're 80% done," saved-progress. Placement: any multi-step flow; show completion state.

**Von Restorff / Isolation Effect** — The distinctive item is remembered and chosen.
- Why: Novelty pops out of a uniform field and captures attention.
- Apply: One CTA color used ONLY for CTAs; highlighted pricing tier; single contrasting badge. Placement: primary CTA everywhere — reserve the accent color for it alone.

**Hick's Law** — More choices = slower, harder decisions.
- Why: Decision time scales with the log of the number of options.
- Apply: One primary CTA per screen, reduce nav links, limit form fields, cap pricing tiers at 3–4. Placement: strip or hide global nav on LPs; single-goal pages.

**Fitts's Law** — Bigger, closer targets are faster/easier to hit.
- Why: Acquisition time scales with distance and inversely with target size.
- Apply: Large tap-friendly CTA buttons, CTA where the thumb/eye already is, generous click area. Placement: mobile CTAs ≥48px tall, full-width or thumb-zone anchored.

**Cognitive Load** — Every element competing for attention taxes the decision.
- Why: Working memory is finite; overload causes abandonment.
- Apply: One message per section, whitespace, scannable bullets, remove non-essential fields/links. Placement: whole page — ruthless subtraction is the highest-leverage CRO move.

**Jakob's Law** — Users expect your site to work like the ones they already know.
- Why: Mental models are built from cumulative experience elsewhere.
- Apply: Cart top-right, logo top-left links home, conventional form/checkout patterns, standard icons. Placement: navigation, checkout, iconography — don't be novel where familiarity converts.

**Peak-End Rule** — Experiences are judged by their peak and their end.
- Why: Memory samples the emotional peak and the final moment, not the average.
- Apply: A delightful confirmation/thank-you page, a strong closing offer + guarantee at page end. Placement: final CTA section and post-conversion screen — don't let the last impression be a dead end.

**Serial-Position (Primacy/Recency)** — First and last items are best recalled.
- Why: Primacy gets rehearsal into long-term memory; recency stays in short-term.
- Apply: Lead with the strongest benefit, close with the second-strongest + CTA. Placement: benefit lists, feature order, pricing sequence.

**Miller's Law** — Working memory holds ~7±2 (practically 3–5) chunks.
- Why: Capacity limits how many items can be held and compared.
- Apply: 3–5 benefit bullets, chunk forms/specs into groups, 3 pricing tiers. Placement: any list, spec table, or step sequence.

**Goal-Gradient Effect** — Effort accelerates as the goal nears.
- Why: Perceived proximity to reward increases motivation.
- Apply: Progress bars that show artificial early progress ("Step 1 of 3 — you've started!"), "2 fields left." Placement: multi-step forms/checkout.

**IKEA Effect** — People value what they helped build.
- Why: Invested effort creates ownership and justification.
- Apply: Configurators, quizzes, "build your quote/plan." Placement: interactive lead-gen module; deliver a personalized result.

**Bandwagon Effect** — People do what they see others doing en masse.
- Why: Popularity signals correctness and safety (related to social proof but momentum-framed).
- Apply: "Join 12,000+," "#1 chosen," trending/most-popular badges, live activity notifications. Placement: hero counter, "Most Popular" pricing tier.

## 2. FRICTION REDUCTION

**Form friction** — every field costs conversions (~a few % per field beyond essentials).
- Remove: Ask only what you'll use now; kill phone if email suffices; use single name field; auto-detect via zip; inline validation (validate on blur, not on submit); enable autofill (`autocomplete` attrs, correct `type`/`inputmode`); multi-step to defer heavy fields; smart defaults; no required account creation for first conversion.

**Choice overload** — too many options → paralysis and exit.
- Remove: One primary CTA per page; 3–4 pricing tiers max; hide/strip nav; recommend a default ("Most Popular"); progressive disclosure for advanced options.

**Cognitive/visual friction** — clutter, low contrast, ambiguity.
- Remove: One idea per section; generous whitespace; clear visual hierarchy (size/weight/color); scannable subheads + bullets; directional cues to the CTA; plain language (8th-grade reading level); no jargon; legible font sizes (16px+ body).

**Trust friction** — doubt about safety, legitimacy, or outcome.
- Remove: Security/SSL badges at payment and forms; visible privacy assurance next to email field ("We never spam"); real contact info + physical address; money-back guarantee; reviews with names/photos; no surprise costs (show shipping/fees early); professional design (design quality IS a trust signal).

## 3. CTA PSYCHOLOGY

**Contrast** — the button must be the most visually distinct element.
- One accent color reserved exclusively for CTAs (Von Restorff); high contrast against background; surrounding whitespace.

**Verb choice** — start with an action verb; lead with the value, not the labor.
- "Get my free quote" > "Submit." "Start saving today" > "Sign up." Avoid friction verbs (Buy, Register) where a benefit verb works.

**First-person** — self-referential copy lifts clicks.
- "Start MY free trial" / "Show ME my estimate" outperforms "your." The visitor reads it as their own inner voice.

**Specificity** — concrete outcome + remove ambiguity about what happens next.
- "Get my quote in 60 seconds" beats "Get started." State time cost, that it's free, and the result.

**Repetition cadence** — repeat the CTA at natural decision points, not randomly.
- Hero → after benefits → after social proof → after pricing/guarantee → final close. Roughly every 1.5–2 screens on long pages. Same primary action; vary supporting microcopy to match the section's context.

**Sticky CTA** — persistent access so intent is never lost.
- Sticky header button (desktop) / sticky bottom bar (mobile) that appears after the hero scrolls out. Keeps the target always one tap away (Fitts's).

**Directional cues** — guide the eye to the CTA.
- Faces/gaze pointing toward the button, arrows, whitespace funnels, contrast gradients, motion. Explicit and implicit cues both work; gaze-following is subconscious.

**Microcopy under CTA** — kill last-second objections at the click point.
- "No credit card required," "Free, cancel anytime," "30-day guarantee," "Takes 60 seconds." This is the single highest-ROI copy real estate.

## 4. TRUST & CREDIBILITY SIGNALS (ranked by typical impact)

1. **Reviews/ratings with names + photos** — highest impact; specificity and faces beat volume. Placement: hero star cluster + testimonial block after primary CTA + snippets beside price.
2. **Guarantees / risk reversal** — money-back, satisfaction, "no obligation." Placement: at/near every CTA and pricing.
3. **Quantified results / numbers** — "12,400 installs," "4.9/5 from 800 reviews," "$2M saved." Concrete numbers out-convert vague claims. Placement: hero and proof strip.
4. **UGC (photos/video from real customers)** — authenticity signal, hard to fake. Placement: gallery mid-page, before/after for visual verticals.
5. **Certifications / licenses / accreditations** — decisive in regulated/high-risk verticals. Placement: trust bar and footer.
6. **"As seen in" / press logos** — borrowed authority. Placement: thin logo strip under hero (label it honestly).
7. **Security/trust badges (SSL, payment marks, BBB)** — reduce transaction anxiety. Placement: at form and payment step specifically.
8. **Customer/partner logos** — social proof for B2B. Placement: under hero.

Rules: put the relevant signal at the moment of doubt (badges at payment, guarantee at price, privacy note at email). Real > polished. One strong specific proof beats five generic ones.

## 5. OFFER & PRICING PSYCHOLOGY

**Charm pricing** — $X9 endings (49, 99) read as meaningfully cheaper.
- Why: Left-digit anchoring — $49 is encoded near "$40," not "$50." Use for value/impulse positioning. For premium/luxury, round numbers ($50, $200) signal quality — match ending to positioning.

**Anchoring / decoy tiers** — present a high anchor first, engineer the target tier.
- 3 tiers; lead visually with premium or place target in the middle with a weak decoy neighbor; label target "Most Popular"; annual anchored against monthly.

**Bundling** — package items so individual prices blur.
- Why: Reduces price comparability and raises perceived value; one price is lower cognitive load. Show "$X value for $Y." Use good/better/best bundles.

**Price framing** — divide to shrink, aggregate to justify.
- "$1/day" (pennies-a-day), "$99/mo" vs "$1,188/yr," "less than your daily coffee." Show savings as both % and $ (whichever is bigger absolute-feeling).

**Guarantees / risk reversal** — transfer risk from buyer to seller.
- Why: Removes the biggest objection (fear of loss). Money-back, free trial, "cancel anytime," "we don't get paid unless…" The stronger and more specific, the higher the lift; place next to price and CTA.

**Free-shipping / spend thresholds** — a threshold nudges basket size.
- Why: Shipping cost is a top abandonment cause; a threshold turns a fee into a goal (goal-gradient). "$12 to free shipping" progress. Show total cost early to avoid checkout-stage shock.

**Decoy / good-better-best** — three options with a clearly dominant target.
- Cap at 3–4 (Hick/Miller); highlight one; differentiate by value not just price.

**Original-price anchoring** — strike-through original next to offer price makes the discount tangible.

## 6. MOBILE-SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR

- **Thumb zones**: Primary actions belong in the natural thumb arc — bottom-center to bottom-right. Top corners are hard to reach one-handed; never put the primary CTA there. Use a sticky bottom CTA bar.
- **Tap targets**: ≥44–48px (Apple 44pt / Google 48dp); ≥8px spacing between tappable elements to prevent mis-taps (Fitts's). Full-width buttons on mobile.
- **Scroll depth**: Most users never reach the bottom — front-load the value prop, primary CTA, and one proof unit above the fold; assume the hero may be the whole experience. Repeat the CTA as they scroll.
- **Load & CWV**: Mobile bounce climbs sharply past ~3s; LCP and CLS directly gate conversion. Compress hero, set explicit image dimensions to prevent layout shift, lazy-load below-fold. (Ref: US Turf LP scored Perf 42 / LCP 9.5s / CLS 0.698 — hero compression + dimensions were the fix.)
- **Input ergonomics**: Correct `inputmode`/`type` to trigger the right keyboard (numeric for phone/zip); minimize typing (dropdowns, toggles, autofill); avoid hover-dependent interactions; single-column forms only.
- **Content density**: Larger fonts (16px+ to avoid iOS zoom), shorter line lengths, collapsible sections (accordions) to manage vertical length without overload.

## 7. MEASUREMENT

**Hypothesis format** (every test starts here):
"Because we observed [data/insight], we believe [change] will cause [effect] for [audience]. We'll measure by [primary metric] and call it a win if [threshold] at 95% significance."

**ICE prioritization**: Score each idea 1–10 on Impact (× traffic × funnel position), Confidence (backed by heatmaps/recordings/analytics/benchmarks), Ease (dev + design + QA). Score = (I+C+E)/3. Execute highest-first. Keep a shared test log so losers become organizational learning.

**What to test first** (highest leverage, in order):
1. Headline / value proposition clarity — biggest single lever.
2. Primary CTA (copy, contrast, placement, microcopy).
3. Hero offer & above-fold layout.
4. Form length / friction (field reduction, multi-step).
5. Social proof presence & placement.
6. Price presentation & guarantee.
Attack the biggest funnel leak (highest absolute drop-off), not the easiest tweak.

**Sample-size sanity**: Calculate required N before launch from baseline conversion rate + minimum detectable effect (MDE). Low-traffic pages can only detect large lifts — don't test subtle changes there; bundle bigger swings. Run a minimum of one full business cycle (7–14 days) to absorb day-of-week/time-of-day variation. Small pages: prioritize big, bold changes over micro-optimizations.

**Avoiding false positives**:
- Don't peek/stop early unless using a proper sequential/Bayesian method — early stopping inflates false-positive rate.
- Fix duration and sample in advance; reach significance AND the pre-set sample AND full-cycle before calling it.
- Correct for multiple comparisons when >2 variants (Bonferroni or equivalent).
- Monitor Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) — a skewed traffic split signals a broken test; discard it.
- Watch guardrail metrics (revenue, bounce, refunds), not just the primary — a CTA-click win that tanks purchases is a loss.
- Novelty/regression effects: fresh designs can spike then fade — validate over the full window.

**Attribution lag** (agency-wide standard): Judge results across 3/7/14/30-day windows before declaring good/bad; never act on a single day or an unsettled window. Conversions back-date — Google back-dates ~7d, Meta uses 7-day-click — so recent windows understate true performance. Wait for the window to settle before reading LP test results tied to downstream conversions.

---

**Meta-principle for the agent**: Subtraction beats addition. The highest-ROI CRO move on most landing pages is removing friction, fields, choices, and clutter — not adding another persuasion tactic. Layer psychology onto a clean, single-goal, fast-loading, trust-saturated page; never use it to paper over a confusing one. Every persuasion signal must be genuine — fake scarcity, fabricated reviews, or dishonest urgency win one session and lose the trust (and legal standing) that compounds conversions over time.
